Bishkek Can't Breathe

Every winter, the capital of Kyrgyzstan disappears under a thick layer of smog. Its air is about 9 times dirtier than the WHO safe level.

Causes

Where does the smog come from?

A study by the Tian Shan Policy Center (AUCA) found that the biggest source of PM2.5 in winter is houses that are not connected to central heating and burn coal in stoves. The CHP power plant is second, and road transport is third.

The city is also in a valley next to the Ala-Too mountains. In cold weather the air does not move, so the smoke stays over the city for days.

Health

What it does to people

PM2.5 particles are so small that they go deep into the lungs and blood. They cause asthma, lung disease and heart problems. Children and old people suffer most.

According to a UNDP study, air pollution in Kyrgyzstan is linked to about 4,100–5,000 deaths every year and cost about $388 million in 2015 (6% of national income).

What can be done?

Clean heating

Connect homes to gas and central heating instead of burning coal.

Cleaner transport

More electric buses, fewer old cars and better public transport.

Monitoring

Air quality sensors across the city so people know when the air is dangerous.
